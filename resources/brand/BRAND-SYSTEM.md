# PFY — Brand & Product Design System v1

**Brand:** Portuguese for You  
**Short name:** PFY  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** APPROVED VISUAL DIRECTION  
**Scope:** Brand identity and product visual language

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the visual direction for **Portuguese for You (PFY)** and establishes the principles that should guide the evolution of the brand and its digital product experience.

PFY is evolving from a content and learning website into a broader educational platform serving:

- students;
- teachers;
- language schools;
- educational organizations;
- institutional and social-impact programs.

The identity must therefore work simultaneously as:

1. an educational brand;
2. a digital product;
3. a platform for different audiences;
4. a contemporary expression of Portuguese language and culture.

The system should create consistency without making every PFY surface look identical.

---

# 2. Brand Idea

## 2.1 Core concept

**Português para a vida real.**

PFY treats Portuguese not only as a subject to be studied, but as a language used to:

- communicate;
- build relationships;
- study;
- teach;
- work;
- travel;
- participate in communities;
- discover cultures;
- create opportunities.

The brand should therefore communicate both **learning** and **connection**.

---

## 2.2 Brand expression

The visual direction can be summarized as:

> **Editorial in the brand. Digital in the product. Human in communication. Brazilian contemporary without clichés.**

This principle should guide future visual decisions.

---

# 3. Brand Personality

PFY should feel:

**Human**  
People and their experiences come before technology.

**Contemporary**  
Modern without following short-lived visual trends.

**International**  
Designed for people from different cultures and backgrounds.

**Educational**  
Clear, trustworthy and useful for learning.

**Warm**  
Welcoming rather than institutional or overly technological.

**Culturally expressive**  
Connected to Portuguese and Brazilian culture without relying on stereotypes.

**Confident**  
PFY should have enough visual presence to operate as a recognizable educational brand.

---

# 4. Visual Territory

The approved visual territory combines:

> **Contemporary Brazilian editorial design × digital product × language and human connection**

The editorial layer creates brand recognition and cultural personality.

The digital layer creates clarity, usability and platform perception.

The human layer connects both.

PFY should not visually resemble either:

- a traditional language school website; or
- a generic SaaS/EdTech application.

The combination of these two worlds is an important part of the identity.

---

# 5. Brand Mark

PFY uses a flexible identity system rather than relying on a single logo configuration.

The system consists of:

### Primary brand

**Portuguese for You**

Used when brand recognition and institutional presence are important.

Typical applications:

- public website;
- campaigns;
- institutional materials;
- presentations;
- certificates;
- partnerships;
- PFY Impact;
- marketing materials.

### Short signature

**PFY**

Used when the full brand has already been established or space is limited.

### Product signature

**pfy**

A compact digital expression intended primarily for product interfaces.

Typical applications:

- application navigation;
- mobile interfaces;
- dashboard sidebar;
- authenticated product environment;
- app icon;
- favicon.

These forms represent the same master brand and must not be treated as separate brands.

# 6. The PFY Wave

The `~` wave is the primary proprietary graphic gesture of the identity.

It should become one of the most recognizable elements of PFY.

## 6.1 Meaning

The wave can represent:

- language;
- conversation;
- connection;
- movement;
- learning journeys;
- progress;
- exchange between people;
- cultural encounters.

It also creates a subtle relationship with the Portuguese language without depending on national symbols.

---

## 6.2 Usage

The wave may appear:

- alongside the wordmark;
- alongside PFY;
- beneath the compact `pfy` mark;
- as a standalone brand gesture;
- in illustrations;
- as a section transition;
- as a visual path;
- in progress-related interactions;
- as an underline;
- in loading or motion states;
- as a framing element in campaigns.

It should not become repetitive decoration.

The wave should communicate movement or connection whenever possible.

# 7. Logo Hierarchy

Preferred hierarchy:

### Institutional / marketing

`Portuguese for You ~`

### Compact brand

`PFY ~`

### Product

`pfy ~`

### Brand gesture

`~`

The full name should normally be introduced before relying heavily on the compact PFY identity in contexts where the audience may not know the brand.

---

# 8. Logo Principles

The identity should maintain:

- generous clear space;
- strong contrast;
- simple compositions;
- consistent proportions;
- legibility at small sizes;
- separation between logo and surrounding content.

Avoid:

- stretching or compressing the mark;
- arbitrary recoloring;
- shadows;
- gradients inside the wordmark;
- outlines;
- excessive effects;
- placing the logo on visually noisy photography without adequate contrast;
- combining the wave with unrelated decorative shapes.

Exact clear-space measurements and minimum sizes must be defined when final production logo assets are created.

Until then, mockup measurements should be treated as visual guidance rather than production specifications.

## 9.2 Important implementation rule

These values are **brand references**, not yet final semantic product tokens.

Before implementation, colors must be validated for:

- WCAG contrast;
- text/background combinations;
- hover states;
- focus states;
- disabled states;
- success;
- warning;
- error;
- informational states;
- charts and data visualization.

The product should not infer semantic meaning directly from brand colors.

For example:

`coral != error`

and:

`yellow != warning`

Brand and semantic color systems must remain conceptually separate.

# 13. Photography

Photography is a central part of the PFY identity.

PFY should show **Portuguese happening in real life**.

Preferred subjects include:

- conversations;
- study situations;
- work;
- travel;
- cultural experiences;
- classrooms;
- teachers;
- independent learners;
- groups;
- everyday environments.

---

## 13.1 Photography style

Prefer:

- natural light;
- documentary/editorial framing;
- authentic environments;
- cultural diversity;
- expressive but believable moments;
- people interacting rather than posing.

Avoid:

- generic corporate stock photography;
- exaggerated expressions;
- artificial classroom scenes;
- stereotypical tourism imagery;
- excessive laptop-focused imagery;
- visual clichés about Brazil.

---

# 14. Brazilian Cultural Expression

Brazilian culture is part of PFY's visual world, but should not become a collection of national clichés.

Avoid making the identity dependent on:

- Brazilian flag colors;
- maps of Brazil;
- carnival imagery;
- tropical iconography used without context;
- football symbolism;
- generic beach imagery.

Instead, Brazilian identity may emerge through:

- architecture;
- everyday environments;
- people;
- food;
- typography;
- language;
- cultural stories;
- contemporary photography;
- regional diversity;
- editorial composition.

The goal is:

> **Brazilian by cultural confidence, not by stereotype.**

---

# 16. Brand vs. Product

PFY intentionally has two visual intensities.

## Brand surfaces

Can be:

- expressive;
- editorial;
- photographic;
- colorful;
- culturally rich.

Examples:

- homepage;
- campaigns;
- institutional pages;
- PFY Impact;
- social communication.

## Product surfaces

Should be:

- calmer;
- more neutral;
- structured;
- highly readable;
- task-oriented.

Examples:

- dashboard;
- activity library;
- course interface;
- student history;
- teacher management;
- organization management.

The product should **feel like PFY without turning every screen into an advertisement for PFY**.

---

# 17. Product Experience Principles

## 17.1 Content first

Learning content and user tasks must remain visually dominant.

Brand elements support them.

---

## 17.2 Progressive complexity

Students, teachers and institutional administrators have different needs.

Interfaces should expose complexity according to the user's context rather than displaying every platform capability simultaneously.

---

## 17.3 Clear hierarchy

Every screen should make clear:

1. where the user is;
2. what they can do;
3. what requires attention;
4. what comes next.

---

## 17.4 Warm but functional

Friendly visual language must not reduce:

- accessibility;
- clarity;
- efficiency;
- information density where required.

---

## 17.5 Consistent platform perception

Students, teachers, schools and organizations should recognize that they are using different parts of the same PFY ecosystem.

# 21. Voice and Visual Communication

PFY communication should be:

- clear;
- conversational;
- encouraging;
- culturally aware;
- direct;
- respectful.

Avoid overly corporate language and exaggerated motivational language.

Preferred communication territory includes ideas such as:

**Português para a vida real.**

# 22. Accessibility

Accessibility is part of the design system rather than a final QA step.

All production UI must consider:

- WCAG color contrast;
- keyboard navigation;
- visible focus states;
- scalable typography;
- readable line lengths;
- sufficient touch targets;
- non-color-only status communication;
- accessible form states;
- reduced-motion preferences;
- semantic structure.

Brand expression must never override accessibility.

# 26. Source-of-Truth Rules

This document owns the **PFY brand and visual direction**.

Implementation specifications may reference this document but should not redefine the brand independently.

Product specifications continue to own:

- behavior;
- workflows;
- permissions;
- data semantics;
- product scope;
- acceptance criteria.

Implementation may adapt visual details where necessary for:

- accessibility;
- responsiveness;
- technical constraints;
- usability.

However, implementation should not silently change established brand principles.

A material change to:

- logo;
- core palette;
- typography;
- visual language;
- audience architecture;
- brand/product relationship;

requires an explicit design/product decision and an update to this document.
